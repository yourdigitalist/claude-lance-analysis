# Market Scan — 2026-07-14 (baseline)

First scan under the lance-market-scan process. This becomes the baseline for future scans. All sources accessed 2026-07-14.

**Method note:** competitor pricing pages block automated fetching from this environment, so pricing below comes from third-party pricing guides and comparison pages found via search (linked per claim), not from the vendors' own pricing pages. Confidence is good but not primary-source. Next scan should re-verify against the vendor pages directly if access allows. Hero messaging could not be captured verbatim this scan and is marked accordingly.

## Summary

- Lance's $29/mo sits comfortably in the solo-tier band of paid all-in-ones ($19–60/mo). The old $20–66 band from the July research still roughly holds, but the edges moved: HoneyBook raised prices sharply (Premium now $129/mo monthly) and 17hats consolidated to a single $60/mo plan with paid add-ons.
- The DIY-stack finding still holds and got sharper: current comparison content actively frames all-in-ones against "Notion + 5-7 subscriptions" workflows. Lance's "one place instead of four tools" pillar matches how the market is already arguing.
- **Differentiator alert (important, nuanced):** no-login client approval links are NOT unique as a capability. A whole point-solution category does exactly this (ClientProof $19/mo, GoVisually, Ashore, SkieShare, Filestage). BUT none of the paid all-in-ones lead with visual file approvals; Bonsai/HoneyBook/Dubsado handle "approval" as contract/proof *sign-off*, not in-context file review. The defensible claim is approvals **inside** the all-in-one, not approvals as such.

### DIY stack (the real competitor)

Still the most common real alternative, and the market now argues against it explicitly: freelancers running client work on Notion typically bolt on 5-7 separate subscriptions (invoicing, time tracking, contracts, portal), sometimes $150-200/mo total. Notion has no invoicing, no billing-connected time tracking, no client portal. Sources: [sales-leads-crm.com](https://www.sales-leads-crm.com/blog/best-alternatives-to-spreadsheets-for-customer-tracking/), [plutio.com freelancer magazine](https://www.plutio.com/freelancer-magazine/best-notion-alternatives-for-freelancers), [lumifyhub.io](https://lumifyhub.io/blog/notion-alternative-for-freelancers). Caveat: several of these sources are competitors' own content marketing — directionally consistent with our July research, but biased narrators; treat the $150-200/mo figure as anecdotal, not citable in ad copy.

## White space

1. **Approvals inside the all-in-one.** Point solutions do no-login approvals well but do nothing else; all-in-ones do everything else but treat approval as a contract signature. Nobody owns "your client reviews the file AND the invoice gets paid, one tool". That's Lance's lane, and it matches the researched pain (scattered feedback + chasing payment being the same person's problem).
2. **Anti-complexity.** Dubsado's loudest negative is 1-2 weeks of setup; HoneyBook's is price hikes. "Working in 10 minutes, $29 flat" is credible white space if true — verify onboarding time before claiming.
3. **Price-hike refugees.** HoneyBook's 89.5% hike generated a documented switcher conversation. Comparison/alternative content targeting it is cheap to test.

## Differentiator status

**"File approvals via no-login link" — ERODED as a standalone claim, HOLDS as a category claim.** Never write "only tool that lets clients approve without logging in" (false; ClientProof et al.). The honest, defensible version: among all-in-one freelancer platforms, Lance builds file approval into the same tool as projects and invoicing. Recommend copy always anchors approvals to the all-in-one context.

## Positioning implications

**No change to /positioning/current.md this scan.** The current framing (differentiator = approvals, positioned against the DIY stack) survives and is strengthened by the tool-fatigue evidence. One sharpening recommended for Marina's decision, not silently applied: the proof-point framing of approvals should be "approvals inside your all-in-one" rather than "no-login approvals" standalone, per the erosion finding above.
